## 1. Overview

We used AI (Claude) in three parts of the project: planning the **layout of our presentation**, getting ideas for **activities and content for our Markdown** (which we then used in the study guide), and getting ideas for **visual design** so the study guide and slides look more attractive.

In all three cases the AI gave us suggestions. It did not produce the final slides or the final study guide. Every suggestion was read by team members, compared with our course materials, and then accepted, changed or dropped before it went into the project. The content in our Markdown files, the slides and the study guide is the team's own work after that checking. The sections below explain each use in detail.

## 2. Use 1 – Help with the layout of the presentation

**What we asked for.** We already had our Markdown content for the three parts of the project (Operating Systems, Software, and Symbolic AI), and we needed to turn it into a presentation. We wanted a deck that is easy for beginners to follow and easy for every team member to edit. We also told the AI that the Operating Systems part should stay lighter than the Software and Symbolic AI parts.

**What the AI suggested.** It suggested how to organise the deck: the order of the sections, how many slides each part should get, and what kind of slide to use where (for example an intro slide, explanation slides, an activity slide and a summary slide). It also suggested keeping a repeating structure and leaving clear placeholders for images and activities, so that different teammates can fill in their own parts without breaking the look of the deck.

**What we did with it.** We did not copy the suggested structure as it was. We compared it with our own Markdown chapters and the assignment requirements, and adjusted the slide order and the number of slides per part so that Software and Symbolic AI get more space than Operating Systems. ## 5. How we checked AI output

For every suggestion we used the same simple process:

1. A team member read the suggestion and compared it with our own materials (lecture notes, course documents, textbook).
2. Anything we could not confirm was changed or left out.
3. The accepted parts were rewritten or built by us in the project files.
4. Another teammate looked at the result before it was kept.
