# Try it: AI or Not AI? Sorting Game (2.1)

**Time:** 15 min · **Group:** groups of 3–4 · **You need:** 3 paper zones and 4 task cards · Study Guide pp. 59–60

**Goal:** decide which tool fits a job: a fixed OS mechanism, symbolic AI rules, or machine learning.

## Prepare

Label three zones on the desk:

- **OS:** a fixed policy or algorithm
- **SYM:** rule-based reasoning that can explain itself
- **ML:** learns from lots of examples

Write the four task cards:

- **Card A:** decide whether sam may open `report.txt`.
- **Card B:** recognise handwritten digits in 10,000 photos.
- **Card C:** guide a technician through a printer-repair procedure and explain each suggestion.
- **Card D:** choose which of 5 ready processes runs next.

## Steps

1. Read each card as a group.
2. Place it in a zone and write **one reason** on a sticky note.
3. If your group disagrees, write down both opinions.

## Reflect

- Which card was hardest to place? Why?
- Does "uses IF-THEN" make something AI?

## Check it: expected answers

| Card | Zone | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | **OS** | A permission check is exact and fast; no reasoning is needed ([1.7](../../../Operating%20Systems%20%E2%80%94%20Essential%20Foundations/07_Security%20and%20Access%20Control/Main%20Theory/07_main-theory.md)). |
| B | **ML** | Handwriting varies too much to write rules by hand. |
| C | **SYM** | A documented procedure can become rules, and the rule trace explains "why". |
| D | **OS** | The scheduler follows a fixed algorithm ([1.3](../../../Operating%20Systems%20%E2%80%94%20Essential%20Foundations/03_Process%20and%20CPU%20Management/Main%20Theory/03_main-theory.md)). |

Other answers are fine if your reason is good. For example, a group might argue that Card C could use ML if there are thousands of past repair records. That shows real thinking about the trade-off.

Using IF-THEN does not make something AI. What matters is whether the system reasons from knowledge, chains conclusions together and can explain them.
