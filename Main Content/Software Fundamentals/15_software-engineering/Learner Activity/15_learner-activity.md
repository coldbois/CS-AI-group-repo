# Try it: Paper Airplane Sprints (2.7)

**Time:** 30 min · **Group:** teams of 4–5 · **You need:** A4 paper, a tape measure, a timer, paper clips · Study Guide pp. 103–104 · Linked Test Bank question: O6

**Goal:** feel the difference between Waterfall and Agile by building paper planes for a "customer".

## Prepare

- The teacher is the **customer**: they want planes that fly far and look cool.
- Each team picks a **tester** (measures every flight) and a **product owner** (talks to the customer).
- Keep the paper clips hidden until the surprise.

## Steps

1. **Waterfall round (8 min).** Plan everything for 3 minutes, build for 5 minutes, and test only at the end. Fly your best plane once.
2. **Agile round:** three 3-minute sprints. After each sprint, fly one plane, show the customer and change your design based on what they say.
3. The tester records every flight distance.
4. **Surprise!** After sprint 2 the customer says: *"Planes must now carry a paper clip!"*

## Reflect

- Which round handled the paper-clip change better? Why?
- What did your tester do that improved the planes?
- When would Waterfall be the better choice?

## Check it: what to look for

- Teams usually improve sprint by sprint, because they get feedback early and can try a new fold each time.
- **Agile copes with the surprise:** you lose one short sprint at most. In Waterfall you find out too late, after the planning and building are finished.
- Testing every round means catching problems early, when they are cheap to fix.
- **Waterfall** suits fixed, well-understood requirements, for example a safety system with strict rules that won't change.
