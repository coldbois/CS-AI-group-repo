**Claims that need a second look when you check sources**
- Section 4.3 says the term "software engineering" became widely used after the 1968 NATO conference, which discussed late, over-budget projects. Check the wording against reference 7 or a reliable history.
- Section 6.1 says the name "waterfall" is often linked to Royce's 1970 paper and that Royce warned against a strict one-pass approach. Read the paper (or a reliable summary) and soften or correct the wording if needed.
- Section 6.4 follows the 2020 Scrum Guide's names (Developers, Product Owner, Scrum Master; sprint of one month or less; 15-minute Daily Scrum). Check that these match the current version of the guide.
- Section 10.1 paraphrases a remark by Edsger Dijkstra that testing shows the presence of bugs, not their absence. Find a primary source for the remark and cite it.
- Section 12.2 gives four maintenance types (corrective, adaptive, perfective, preventive). Confirm the definitions against reference 1.
- Section 12.3 describes technical debt with a loan analogy. Add a source for the term (it is often credited to Ward Cunningham) before using the attribution.
- Section 13.2 paraphrases Brooks's observation. Confirm in reference 10.
- Section 9.4 shows real Git output (Git 2.43). Output wording and commit IDs may differ slightly in other versions.
