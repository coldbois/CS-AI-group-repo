

The developed material must include:

· Explanations: Accurate, beginner-friendly explanations of the main concepts.

· Terminology: Definitions of unfamiliar terms when they first appear.

· A worked example or demonstration: A step-by-step illustration showing how an idea works.

· A meaningful visual where useful: A diagram, table, annotated image, or other visual that helps explain the content.

· A learner activity or application question: Something that requires learners to use the idea.

· An answer or expected outcome: An explanation that allows the learner and teacher to check understanding.

· References: Identifiable sources supporting the content and any borrowed material.

Use headings, lists, tables, images, and code blocks appropriately. Prioritize clear content and organization over decorative formatting.

Do not simply paste textbook passages, previous students’ work, or AI output into Markdown. Check the material, organize it for your learners, and be prepared to explain it.
