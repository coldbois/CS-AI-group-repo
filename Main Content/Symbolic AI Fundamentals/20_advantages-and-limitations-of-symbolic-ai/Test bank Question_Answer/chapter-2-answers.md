**Chapter:** Advantages and Limitations of Symbolic AI

Use this file after attempting the questions. The explanations are intended to teach, not simply mark an answer.

## Section A — True / False Answers

1. **False.** Consistency does not guarantee that the rules or input data are correct.
2. **True.** A developer can often inspect which rules led to a conclusion.
3. **False.** Rules can be supplied directly; the system still needs accurate knowledge and testing.
4. **True.** Capturing expertise as usable facts and rules can be difficult.
5. **False.** Basic crisp rules are limited for uncertain evidence; probabilistic or fuzzy extensions may help.
6. **True.** The system is limited by its encoded knowledge and mechanisms.
7. **False.** Raw image pattern recognition is usually better served by machine-learning/computer-vision methods or a hybrid.

## Section B — Multiple-Choice Answers

1. **B — They can make reasoning paths easier to inspect.** Explicit rules can make a conclusion traceable.
2. **B — It applies the same defined rules in the same way to equivalent cases.** Consistency concerns repeated application, not the correctness of the rules.
3. **A — The difficulty of collecting and encoding specialist knowledge.** Expertise can be difficult to elicit and represent as usable facts and rules.
4. **B — They are easiest to apply when conditions are crisp and known.** Basic yes/no rules do not naturally capture degrees of belief or ambiguous evidence.
5. **A — Rules can interact and create exceptions or conflicts.** Large rule bases can be difficult to maintain and test.
6. **C — Recognizing objects directly from raw photographs.** Raw image recognition typically requires a perception approach such as computer vision.
7. **B — Consistency means repeated rule application; correctness means the rules and data fit the task.** A system can consistently apply incorrect or incomplete rules.

## Section C — Open-ended Marking Guidance

Answers may vary. Award credit for accurate concepts, a clear explanation, a relevant example and reasoned evaluation. The points below are key elements, not the only acceptable wording.

### Question 1

- Distinguish consistency from correctness.
- Identify missing policy conditions, such as other eligibility criteria.
- Explain how poor, outdated or incomplete facts/rules can misclassify applicants.
- Discuss rule validation, exception handling, testing and maintenance.

### Question 2

- Discuss at least three accurate strengths: traceability, consistent rule application, structured reasoning, no large training set for hand-written rules, and direct rule updates.
- Discuss at least three limitations: knowledge acquisition, uncertainty, novel cases, unstructured inputs, rule interactions and maintenance.
- Use one example to show the trade-off and explain the conditions under which the system is useful.
- Avoid absolute claims such as “symbolic AI can never handle uncertainty.”
