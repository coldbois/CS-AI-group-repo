## Section B — Multiple-Choice Answers

1. **B — Representing knowledge explicitly and reasoning with symbols and rules.** Symbolic AI uses explicit representations such as facts, rules and relationships.
2. **B — A stored piece of represented information.** A fact is information represented as true in the system model.
3. **C — Apply reasoning procedures to facts and rules.** The inference engine applies rules to derive conclusions.
4. **B — Forward chaining.** Forward chaining starts with facts and applies matching rules.
5. **A — Backward chaining.** Backward chaining works from a goal towards supporting conditions.
6. **A — The 1956 Dartmouth Summer Research Project.** The Dartmouth project in 1956 is a foundational event in AI history.
7. **B — A knowledge graph of entities and relationships.** Knowledge graphs represent entities and their relationships.

## Section C — Open-ended Marking Guidance

Answers may vary. Award credit for accurate concepts, a clear explanation, a relevant example and reasoned evaluation. The points below are key elements, not the only acceptable wording.

### Question 1

- Define symbolic representation and distinguish a fact from a rule.
- Explain the knowledge base as the stored facts/rules and the inference engine as the component that applies reasoning.
- Give a valid example with at least two facts and one rule, then show the derived conclusion.
- Mention that the result is limited by the supplied facts, rules and assumptions.

### Question 2

- For explicit policy conditions, discuss symbolic rules and their traceability/consistency.
- For image recognition, explain why learned visual pattern models are normally more suitable than hand-writing every visual rule.
- Compare data/knowledge requirements, explainability and maintenance.
- Recognize that a hybrid may use perception plus symbolic constraints or reasoning.
